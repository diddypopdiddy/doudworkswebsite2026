Facilitator script

This is how the lesson runs in a real classroom. The app follows the same order.

  1. Walk in and keep it vague. Do you guys want to play a game? Don't mention AI rules, integrity, or grading.
  2. Students open the game, tap Yes, and enter a nickname and the class code.
  3. Start Round 1. Say only: Write the best paragraph on this topic. You have five minutes. Use the chatbot. No more instructions. Most students will ask it to write the paragraph and then sit back.
  4. When time's up, screens lock. Hands off. Switch seats with the person next to you.
  5. (Solo mode skips the seat swap. Each student grades their own chat or taps “grade it for me.”) Each screen shows two example conversations, one offloading and one tutor-style. Ask: Which one is cheating, and which one is learning? What's different? Then everyone taps Show the points.
  6. Students see the rubric and grade the classmate's chat they're sitting at, message by message. (The optional AI referee can settle arguments.)
  7. The scores land. Most are negative (−20, −30). Let the groans happen. Students go back to their own seat and see their breakdown.
  8. Reteach in one line: AI is a feedback tool, not a writing tool. You write, and it helps you get better. The screen shows the three moves that earn points.
  9. Start Round 2. Same topic, new blank chat, same clock. Now they bring their own sentences: Here's my opening. Don't rewrite it. What do you think?
  10. Call time, swap and grade again, then hand back. Everyone sees their comeback.
  11. Each student lands on a practice level: Passenger → Learner → AI Permit → AI License. It's a conversation starter about the pathway, not an official credential.
  12. Crown the winner. Put the crown screen on the projector: top score, biggest comeback, and how many students reached each level.

Privacy and limits

OpenAI processes messages for live replies and referee grading. The game saves the conversation in the student's browser. Use New player to clear it from a shared device. The class roster shows nicknames and scores to the teacher who created the room. Rooms close after eight hours. Use End game to clear a roster immediately. Ask students to use a nickname and keep personal information out of the chat.
