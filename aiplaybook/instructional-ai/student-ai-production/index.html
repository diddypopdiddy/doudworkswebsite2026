Instructional AI / plan, produce, verify

Student AI production

Students use AI through a visible production process: plan before prompting, direct the work, verify the output, publish useful evidence, and reflect on their decisions.

Human check

What is the learning goal? What must the person still decide?

01

Choose the AI or no-AI lane

Begin with the learning goal. Decide what the student must think, know, create, or perform independently; then decide whether AI belongs and what kind of support is appropriate.

02

The Production Spiral

Student AI use follows a repeatable five-stage workflow. Each project builds the judgment needed for the next one.

Pre-Production → Production → Post-Production → Publish → Reflect
03

Pre-Production — plan before prompting

  • Define the task or problem and the intended skill or action.
  • Choose the planned AI Mode or Modes and explain why they fit.
  • State what AI may contribute and what remains the student’s responsibility.
  • Gather the sources, context, constraints, and privacy-safe inputs the AI will need.
  • Draft at least one starting prompt.
  • Identify the verification route before using AI.
  • Check ethics, data, privacy, and possible harm before anything is shared.
04

Production — think with AI

  • Prompt, question, compare, draft, test, and revise instead of requesting a finished answer.
  • Switch AI Modes when the kind of work changes.
  • Keep meaningful prompts, responses, choices, rejected output, drafts, and revisions.
  • Pause for explanation, peer talk, source use, and independent thinking.
  • Keep the student directing the work and responsible for the content and quality.
05

Post-Production — verify and challenge

  • Check claims, sources, logic, calculations, quotations, examples, and media.
  • Compare the output with course materials, primary sources, trusted references, or human expertise.
  • Look for bias, missing perspectives, oversimplification, privacy problems, and unsupported confidence.
  • Correct the work and document the changes that matter.
  • Make sure the student can explain and defend the final choices without the chatbot.
06

Publish — share the work and useful evidence

  • Submit or share the final artifact in the format the task requires.
  • Include the requested process evidence: a curated chatlog, selected screenshots, before-and-after work, or a short process note.
  • Explain what AI contributed, what the student decided, and how important output was checked.
  • Protect private information when sharing any conversation or evidence.
07

Reflect — feed the next project

  • Where did AI help, interfere, or create extra work?
  • What did the student accept, reject, revise, or override?
  • Which AI Modes were used, and did the sequence fit the task?
  • What can the student now explain, create, or perform independently?
  • What should change in the next Pre-Production plan?
08

Five Modes inside the workflow

  • Idea Mode: surface possibilities, questions, or gaps before choosing a direction.
  • Learning Mode: seek explanations, examples, questions, or comparisons that build understanding.
  • Feedback Mode: critique work already begun without replacing the student’s draft.
  • Tool Mode: organize, transform, format, or build under clear student direction.
  • Practice Mode: rehearse, simulate, test, or defend thinking beyond a first response.
09

Four Core Competencies stay on

  • Prompting: make the goal, context, sources, constraints, and AI role clear.
  • Data & Privacy: minimize information and remove protected or identifiable data.
  • Ethics: consider permission, authorship, fairness, access, bias, and possible harm.
  • Verification: plan the check, test important output, and show what changed.
10

Student-centered still means human-centered

AI should create more room for student choice, explanation, peer teaching, hands-on work, productive struggle, and teacher response—not less. Strong AI-supported work ends with stronger human understanding and capability.
