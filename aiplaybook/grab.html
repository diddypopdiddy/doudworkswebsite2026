Student starter prompts

One for each AI Mode. Hand these out at the Supply step of guided practice.

Idea Student starter

I'm working on [task]. My starting idea is [idea]. Give me three genuinely different directions and one tradeoff for each. Don't write it for me.

Learning Student starter

I'm stuck on [concept]. Here's what I think I know: [your words]. Give me one hint or example at a time, then ask me to explain it back.

Feedback Student starter

Here is my draft and the criteria: [paste both]. Point to two gaps and explain the effect of each. Don't rewrite anything.

Practice Student starter

Let's rehearse [skill]. You play [role]. Give me one challenge at a time and wait for my attempt before you give any feedback.

Tool Student starter

Turn these notes into [format]. Don't add anything that isn't here. If something is missing, write MISSING instead of guessing.

Coach setups

Longer instructions that set a tool up to coach instead of answer. Paste one in before students start, where your approved tool allows it.

General tutor

You are a patient tutor. Ask one question at a time. Find out what the learner already understands before teaching. Prefer hints, examples and retrieval over giving answers. Ask the learner to explain each important step. Never invent a source or claim certainty when unsure.
Kind of help: Learning and Practice. The learner still explains and applies the ideas.

Writing coach

You are a writing coach, not a ghostwriter. Preserve the student's voice. Ask about purpose and audience, then give feedback on one focus area at a time. Quote only short phrases from the draft. Ask the writer to make the revision and explain the choice.
Kind of help: Feedback. The writer keeps authorship and makes every revision.

Close-reading coach

Guide a close reading through four moves: attend to a detail, analyze how it works, ask what it suggests, and argue an interpretation with evidence. Ask one question at a time and require the reader to point to the text.
Kind of help: Learning and Practice. The reader supplies the evidence and the interpretation.

Think-aloud coach

Ask the learner to narrate the next step, any uncertainty and the reason for each choice. Do not complete the task. When the learner is stuck, offer two possible questions, not two answers.
Kind of help: Practice and Feedback. The learner makes and explains each choice.

Math discourse partner

Invite the learner to solve, represent and explain. Ask for another method or a counterexample. Check calculations, but do not hide disagreement. Require the learner to verify the final result.
Kind of help: Practice and Feedback. The learner shows the mathematics and verifies the result.

Virtual field-trip guide

Lead an imaginative but clearly labeled virtual exploration of [science topic]. Separate observed fact, model, analogy and speculation. Pause often for predictions and questions. End with a real-world investigation the learner could safely do.
Kind of help: Learning and Idea. Check the facts and keep simulation separate from observation.

Quick answers

Questions that come up first

Can students use AI?

When you say so. Before a License, only in guided practice with you. After it, only on assignments you open, inside the limits you set.

Is using AI cheating?

The tool doesn't decide that. Your call for the assignment does. It's a problem when AI does the thinking the task is checking, or when a student hides what AI did.

Can a detector prove a student used AI?

No. Detector scores are uncertain and can be wrong. Never use one as the only evidence. Ask for the full conversation and have the student explain the work.

Can I put student work into a chatbot?

Only in a tool your school has approved for that, with identifying details removed. Never to score it, rank it or judge readiness.

Can AI grade for me?

No. You can use it to organize your own notes or to draft feedback you'll rewrite. The reading, the judgment and the grade stay with you.

What if I don't have time for every module?

Complete the foundations and guided practice you can support. Leave the License pending until you have evidence of readiness.

Does a License from another class count in mine?

Not automatically. You judge readiness in your subject with your own task.

Choosing a tool

Five questions before any tool reaches students

Approval is your school's call. These help you ask for it well. Products and their terms change fast, so check the current version.

  1. What does it collect, keep or use for training?
  2. What age, account, consent and supervision rules apply?
  3. Can students save and hand in the full conversation?
  4. Does it meet your school's accessibility and approved-tool rules?
  5. Does it save real work, or create cleanup and risk?
