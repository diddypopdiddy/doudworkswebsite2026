Module 6 · When needed

When something feels off

A suggested pace. Stretch or combine days to fit your class.

At a glance
Time
About 10 minutes with the student

Goal

Respond to a concern using the work, the full task conversation and the student’s explanation.

Review a concern

  1. First

    Ask to see the process

    Ask privately and in the tone you use for a routine check.

    Say thisWalk me through how you got here. Can I see the conversation for this task?
  2. Then

    Read the evidence

    Review the full conversation, drafts, sources and other work against the assignment’s stated permission. Identify what you can confirm and what evidence is missing.

  3. Next

    Ask for an explanation

    Ask the student to identify the help they used, show a decision and explain a check. Offer an accessible way to demonstrate the learning.

    Say thisWhich suggestion did you change or leave out? What did you use to check the claim?
  4. Finally

    Record the evidence and act

    Write down the evidence and use the school’s process for a learning, integrity or privacy concern.

Choose a response

A skill needs practice

Name the specific gap, run targeted practice and collect fresh evidence.

There is evidence of a broken task rule

Follow your school’s academic integrity process and its safeguards. Give the student an opportunity to respond.

Tool access or data permission is unresolved

Pause live use and follow school procedure before resuming.

Keep the check fair

  • Use actual work, process and explanation as evidence. A detector score or change in writing style can be wrong and cannot establish misconduct.
  • Review student work in approved school systems. Keep it out of outside tools used to guess authorship.
  • If a conversation is missing, ask what happened and arrange a way for the student to show the learning.
  • Check some conversations routinely so students know what being pulled over involves.
