AI integrity / make thinking visible

Show your AI process

AI-supported work does not always need a conventional citation. It does need enough process evidence to understand the AI contribution and the human decisions.

Human check

What is the learning goal? What must the person still decide?

01

Disclosure, not citation theater

The purpose is not to imitate a bibliography when that does not help anyone understand the work. The purpose is to make the important interaction, contribution, checks, and human decisions visible.

02

Process Evidence

Process Evidence shows how you used AI: what AI contributed, how you directed it, what you checked, revised, or rejected, and which final decisions remained yours. Keep the record that fits the task.

03

Turn in the conversation

  • A full conversation when every turn is relevant and safe to share.
  • A curated chatlog with the meaningful prompts, responses, and annotations.
  • Selected screenshots or an exported excerpt.
  • A before-and-after artifact that shows what changed.
04

Protect privacy before sharing

Remove personal, student, school, health, disability, grade, account, and other protected information from prompts and submitted chat evidence. Do not create a public share link when the conversation contains private context.

05

Talk through the work

A brief conference, presentation, or oral explanation can reveal more than a formal citation. Be ready to explain the chosen AI Mode, the important prompt decisions, what you accepted or rejected, and how you verified the result.

06

A short process note

  • Task and learning goal
  • AI Mode used for the task
  • Key prompts or conversation turns
  • What AI contributed
  • What you kept, changed, or rejected
  • How important claims were verified
  • What remained your decision or authorship
07

When formal citation applies

A teacher, publication, research context, or style guide may still require a formal citation. Follow that expectation when it applies, but remember that a citation alone does not address accuracy, bias, privacy, permission, or assignment fit.
