AI integrity / define the job

Prompting

Prompting is an AI integrity practice: choose the right kind of support, give it a clear job, and plan how you will check the result.

Human check

What is the learning goal? What must the person still decide?

01

Choose the AI Mode before the prompt

  • Idea Mode: surface possibilities or gaps.
  • Learning Mode: explain, question, or compare.
  • Feedback Mode: critique work already begun.
  • Tool Mode: organize, transform, build, or produce under human direction.
  • Practice Mode: rehearse, simulate, or stress-test.
02

Prompting is an integrity practice

Your words shape the conditions of the response. A good prompt names the learning goal, the appropriate AI role, the relevant context, what must remain human, and the boundaries the model should respect.

03

Weak prompts create weak conditions

A vague prompt invites generic output. A prompt with hidden assumptions can reproduce bias. A prompt that asks for unsupported certainty can invite invented facts or sources. Prompt quality does not guarantee truth, but it makes the purpose, limits, and responsibility easier to inspect.

04

Weak prompt / stronger prompt

  • Weak: Write my essay about school uniforms.
  • Stronger: I am preparing an argument about school uniforms. Ask me three questions about my position and evidence. Then identify one counterargument I should investigate. Do not draft the essay or invent sources.
05

A practical structure

  • Role or perspective
  • Task and desired outcome
  • Relevant context or sources
  • Constraints and boundaries
  • Output format
  • A request for uncertainty, checks, or questions
06

Identify verification before use

Decide how you can check the response before you prompt. Name the course text, primary source, calculation, trusted reference, expert, or human observation that can test the important claims.

07

Use all four Core Competencies

  • Prompting: make the goal, context, constraints, and AI role clear.
  • Data & Privacy: remove protected or identifiable information.
  • Ethics: inspect permission, authorship, access, bias, and possible harm.
  • Verification: use the route you identified and document what changed.
08

Iterate visibly

Treat the first response as material. Ask what is missing, compare alternatives, add evidence, and revise with your own judgment. Keep enough process to explain what changed.
