AI Production Teacher Playbook

Teacher key · AI Literacy

Use with the numbered student copy. Accept equivalent answers, then use the objective beside each item to plan reteaching.

  1. Objective 1 · Vocabulary

    A music app proposes a playlist based on what you played last week. Which action is it taking? A. Classify   B. Recommend   C. Generate   D. Translate

    B. Recommend. It proposes something the listener might want.

  2. Objective 1 · Vocabulary

    What is a prompt? A. The input or request you give a model   B. The list of examples it learned from   C. A prediction score

    A. A prompt is the input or request given to the model.

  3. Objective 1 · Vocabulary

    What do we call the examples a model learns from? A. Prompts   B. Training data   C. Tokens

    B. Training data. Those are the examples used during training.

  4. Objective 1 · Vocabulary

    Which describes a token? A. A whole conversation   B. A source citation   C. A piece of text the model works with

    C. A token is a piece of text. It can be a word, part of a word, or punctuation.

  5. Objective 2 · Patterns and predictions

    A model trained on labeled photos puts a new picture in the ‘recycling bin’ category. Which action did it take? A. Train   B. Classify   C. Generate

    B. Classify. It assigned a label to an example it had never seen.

  6. Objective 4 · Limits and checking

    A chatbot writes a confident answer. Its confidence alone can establish that the answer is correct. ________

    Myth. Tone cannot confirm a factual claim. Check the claim against evidence.

  7. Objective 2 · Patterns and predictions

    A classifier that was only shown blue mugs might struggle to identify a red mug. ________

    Fact. It may have learned color as a shortcut because its training examples lacked variety.

  8. Objective 4 · Limits and checking

    A model can generate a source title that sounds real even when the source cannot be found. ________

    Fact. A plausible title can be generated without a real source behind it.

  9. Objective 3 · History

    Put these in the order from our class timeline. Generative AI, rules written by people, modern deep neural networks, early machine learning.

    Rules written by people → early machine learning → modern deep neural networks → generative AI. This is a simplified classroom timeline.

  10. Objective 4 · Limits and checking

    Give one reason to check an AI answer and name a source or action you could use to check it.

    Accept a specific risk and a matching check. For example, a bus schedule can be out of date, so check the transit agency’s current timetable. A calculation can be wrong, so redo it by hand.
