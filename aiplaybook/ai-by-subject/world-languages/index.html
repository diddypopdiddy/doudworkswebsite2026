World languages

Quick ideas for using AI for language practice—without replacing real communication.

Jump to
Ideas to tryWhat students still doWatch outQuick check
01

Ideas to try

Student usePractice Mode

Rehearse a conversation

Practice a teacher-approved scenario at the learner’s level, then use key phrases in a real conversation.

Start hereTarget words or structures, a clear scenario, and a first attempt at a response.

Student useFeedback Mode

Receive one correction at a time

Ask for one explained correction to a student-written sentence—not a full rewrite.

Start hereThe student’s own attempt and one focus such as tense, tone, or word order.

Student useLearning Mode

Clarify grammar, tone, or vocabulary

Compare examples or ask for an explanation, then check it against class materials or authentic use.

Start hereA specific sentence or point of confusion and a trusted source for checking.

02

What students still do

Students make meaning, listen, speak, write, and communicate independently. Teachers protect accuracy, culture, and context.

03

Watch out

  • AI can use the wrong register, invent an idiom, or flatten culture and dialect.
  • Translation can quietly replace the language students are supposed to build.
  • Skip AI when: the goal is live conversation, independent speaking or writing, or seeing the student’s current proficiency.
04

Quick check

  • Check class materials, trusted dictionaries, authentic examples, and teacher or fluent-speaker feedback.
  • Save: first attempts, practice turns, corrections, source checks, and later independent speaking or writing.
