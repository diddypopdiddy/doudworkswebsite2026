Module 2 · Days 3–6

The four AI Core Competencies

A suggested pace. Stretch or combine days to fit your class.

Printable lesson planJump to the lesson flow

Total time
Four 45-minute lessons, plus the suggested quiz
Live student tool
None. Browser stations or paper.
AI Core Competencies in the Framework

Goal

Students can apply all four AI Core Competencies together, on paper, before they open a live tool.

Learning objectives

  1. Students will be able to use Prompting to fit the help to the goal, context and boundary, and redirect it when needed.
  2. Students will be able to apply Data & Privacy by choosing minimal approved input and stopping when approval is uncertain.
  3. Students will be able to apply Ethics by following the assignment’s permission and describing AI’s help honestly.
  4. Students will be able to use Verification to sort important claims as supported, contradicted or not established.

Key vocabulary

AI Core Competencies
Four skills used together whenever you work with AI.
Prompting
Your words matter. Direct AI to give fitting help, and adjust the request if the response doesn’t fit.
Data & Privacy
Some information should not go into AI. Review your actual input and share only what is necessary and approved.
Ethics
AI is appropriate sometimes and not other times. Follow the task’s limits, own your choices and describe help truthfully.
Verification
AI can be wrong. Check important claims and results against evidence you can trust.
Assignment boundary
The line between what AI may do and what you are meant to do yourself.

Lesson flow

Pick today’s lesson. Each day uses the same 45-minute shape with its own station, example and wording.

Day 3 · Prompting

Opening

  1. 5 min
    Name today’s Core Competency: Prompting
    Teacher
    Say it plainly first: “Your words matter.” Then put one short assignment and its permission on the board. Teach today’s words: goal (what you need), context (what helps AI respond), boundary (what the tool may do).
    Students
    Underline the task’s learning target and name one decision you would make before using AI.
    Say thisWhat are you learning here? What would you have to decide for yourself?

Teach

  1. 7 min
    Think aloud through one example
    Teacher
    Model this decision: ask for questions about an unclear pronoun when the reasoning must stay yours. Point to the line or source that supports it.
    Students
    Explain to a partner why the decision fits the task.
    Say thisHere’s the information I used to make that choice. Which detail mattered?

Practice

  1. 20 min
    Prompt Ladder
    Teacher
    Open Prompt Ladder on student devices or the projector. Ask for reasons while students practice. Leave a little time for a second attempt.
    Students
    Rank opening prompts and explain which one gives fitting help. Compare your reason with a partner and revise one decision if needed.
    Say thisGive AI its job and its limits. What part will you do yourself?

    No-tech version Put “Write my essay about school uniforms” on the board. Have students rewrite it to ask for questions about their position and evidence. Keep the writing and choice of sources with the student.

    On paper Foundations Student Workbook, pp. 3–13.

Check

  1. 8 min
    Compare evidence
    Teacher
    Revisit two decisions from practice. Look for this: they give a goal and a boundary, explain why the help fits, and redirect when it drifts.
    Students
    Defend one choice using a direction or source and explain what you would change in a different situation.
    Say thisPoint to the part of the task or source that supports your choice.

Exit

  1. 5 min
    Exit with a decision and reason
    Teacher
    Collect a response to a fresh short example. Use it to open the next day.
    Students
    Write what you would do and one piece of evidence for that decision.
    Say thisI’m looking for your choice and the reason you can defend.

Teach

  1. 7 min

    Teacher

    Students

    Say thisHere’s the information I used to make that choice. Which detail mattered?

Day 5 · Ethics

  1. 5 min

    Teacher
    Say it plainly first: “AI is appropriate sometimes and not other times.” Then put one short assignment and its permission on the board. Teach today’s words: permission (what the task allows), ownership (thinking and choices you make), disclosure (a truthful account of AI’s help).
    Students
    Underline the task’s learning target and name one decision you would make before using AI.
    Say thisWhat are you learning here? What would you have to decide for yourself?

Teach

  1. 7 min
    Think aloud through one example
    Teacher
    Model this decision: decline an AI-written interpretation when the assignment allows only layout feedback. Point to the line or source that supports it.
    Students
    Explain to a partner why the decision fits the task.
    Say thisHere’s the information I used to make that choice. Which detail mattered?

Practice

  1. 20 min
    Fence Line
    Teacher

    Students

    Say thisWhich thinking is this task checking? Tell me which part AI helped with.

    No-tech version Read a short task with a rule and a scenario. Have students stand under Allowed, Ask first or Not allowed and cite the part of the task that supports their answer.

Day 6 · Verification

Opening

  1. 5 min
    Name today’s Core Competency: Verification
    Teacher
    Say it plainly first: “AI can be wrong.” Then put one short assignment and its permission on the board. Teach today’s words: claim (something that can be checked), supported (evidence backs it), contradicted (evidence conflicts), not established (evidence is insufficient).
    Students
    Underline the task’s learning target and name one decision you would make before using AI.
    Say thisWhat are you learning here? What would you have to decide for yourself?

Teach

  1. 7 min
    Think aloud through one example
    Teacher
    Model this decision: compare each table row with the original notes, preserve missing values and work out the subtotal yourself. Point to the line or source that supports it.
    Students
    Explain to a partner why the decision fits the task.
    Say thisHere’s the information I used to make that choice. Which detail mattered?

Practice

  1. 20 min
    Fact Detective
    Teacher
    Open Fact Detective on student devices or the projector. Ask for reasons while students practice. Leave a little time for a second attempt.
    Students
    Find the checkable claim and say where you would check it. Compare your reason with a partner and revise one decision if needed.
    Say thisShow me the source or the calculation that lets you keep that claim.

    No-tech version Give pairs a short teacher-written AI-style paragraph and a source sheet. Include a correct claim, a contradicted claim and a detail the source cannot settle. Ask what to correct or hold back.

    On paper Foundations Student Workbook, pp. 14–23.

Check

  1. 8 min
    Put all four together at the Crossroads
    Teacher
    Open the Crossroads, a scholarship-essay scenario with four decisions. Discuss why all four Core Competencies matter.
    Students
    Defend one choice using a direction or source and explain what you would change in a different situation.
    Say thisLook at the same situation through all four Core Competencies. Which action would you take first, and why?

Check for understanding

Suggested quiz

12 items · About 12–15 minutes. Use this for ungraded, formative feedback. A quiz result does not issue a Permit.

Printable student copy
Preview the questions

  1. Your group needs two layout options for a display. The class rules reserve the wording and layout choice for you. Write one prompt that gives AI a useful job.

  2. You ask a tool to pose one rehearsal question. It gives you the whole answer. What is your next message?

  3. You ask “Help me with my presentation.” Your audience is new to the topic and you only want ideas for the opening. Name two details that would make the request fit.

  4. An approved tool may sort sample supply lists. Your real list contains a classmate’s phone number and notes about who borrows supplies. What should you give the tool?

  5. Your school approved a chat tool for class. You can’t tell if it approved the new file-upload feature. What do you do before uploading?

  6. The directions allow AI to format a table. The tool offers to write your conclusion too. What should you do?

  7. You kept two suggestions from AI feedback but wrote your own revision. Write one sentence that tells the teacher how you used it.

  8. The supplied schedule says the event starts at 4:00. AI writes that it starts at 4:30. Classify the claim and say what to do.

  9. A floor plan marks two entrances. AI claims both have automatic doors. The plan shows no door details. Classify the claim and say what to do.

  10. The original inventory lists three boxes of four brushes. An AI table says 12 brushes total. Classify the total and show the check.

  11. You’re allowed to get feedback on a club poster. The draft includes someone’s private email, and the tool asks to rewrite the whole poster. Name two Core Competencies that apply and one action for each.

  12. AI gives you an unlisted cost and a finished recommendation after you asked for budget feedback only. Name two Core Competencies that apply and one action for each.

Independent foundation check

After the four lessons and integrated practice, use Form A or Form B and the corresponding fresh transfer card from the Foundations Teacher Guide and Keys. Release only the current student materials. Keep keys, future cases and alternate forms with you.

Score each AI Core Competency independently using Not yet, With guidance or Independent. Record the privacy gate and the assignment boundary gate separately as Pass, Not yet or Not observed. Use Independent evidence across all four and a Pass on both gates for foundation readiness. Reteach and collect fresh evidence where a row or gate is unresolved.

The Permit stage includes eligibility, foundations and guided practice. Confirm the approved service, account and evidence hand-in before live student use.

Classroom kit list

Misconceptions and reteach

“I gave it a clear prompt. I’m done.”
Say thisLook at the response. Does it still fit our goal and boundary?

Redo Have students repair a prompt after the tool offers more help than the task allows.

“I erased the name. The rest is fine.”
Say thisLook at how the details work together. What is the smallest approved input that will do the job?

Redo Re-sort an invented note and supply a minimal substitute.

“I have a License, so AI is allowed.”
Say thisRead the directions for this activity first.

Redo Give two different assignment directions and ask students to justify what help is allowed on each.

“The source doesn’t say it’s wrong, so it must be true.”
Say thisA source can leave a claim unsettled. What evidence would we need?

Redo Sort the claims again and explain which to use, fix or hold back.
