Module 2 · Days 2–5

The four AI Core Competencies

A suggested pace. Stretch or combine days to fit your class.

MaterialsLesson flowCheck for understanding
At a glance
Time
Four 45-minute periods · two-period rotation option
Live tool
No student chatbot

Goal

Students can apply all four AI Core Competencies together, on paper, before they open a live tool.

Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Make AI help fit the goal, context and boundary, and redirect when needed (Prompting).
  2. Choose minimal approved input and pause to ask when approval is uncertain (Data & Privacy).
  3. Follow the assignment permission and truthfully describe AI’s help (Ethics).
  4. Sort important claims as supported, contradicted or not established (Verification).

Key vocabulary

Prompting
Give the goal, useful approved context, the AI role and the kind of response you need. Test the fit and redirect.
Data & Privacy
Choose the smallest useful set of approved inputs. Check both the content and where it is going.
Ethics
Respect task permission, keep ownership of the assigned thinking and describe the help honestly.
Verification
Trace material claims to sources and test important operations with a check you can explain.

Lesson flow

Teach one competency per day using the timing below. On the last day, use 13 minutes of practice for Verification and 15 minutes for the suggested quiz. Close by revisiting how the four competencies worked together.

For two periods, model and rotate through the four stations in period one (8 minutes each). In period two, use the Crossroads to apply all four, then give the suggested quiz and review an example.

Prompting

Vocabulary. Goal, context, boundary, response

Prompt Ladder · Framework example

On paper. Put three prompts for the same task on slips. Rank their fit, then rewrite one to name what the student must do.

Example. Ask for questions about an unclear pronoun when the reasoning must stay yours.

Look for. They can explain why a prompt fits and repair it when the reply crosses the task boundary.

Say thisTell it what help you need and what thinking stays yours.

Data & Privacy

Vocabulary. Minimal input, approved tool, identifying details

Keep It Out · Framework example

On paper. Give students a fictional message with a name, a schedule and a general question. Circle what the tool actually needs.

Example. Use a made-up misconception when planning reteaching. The AI needs the misconception, and the student record stays in school systems.

Look for. They can name what to leave out and pause to ask when approval is uncertain.

Say thisCheck the actual content before you send anything.

Ethics

Vocabulary. Permission, ownership, disclosure

Fence Line · Framework example

On paper. Give groups a task that allows layout feedback and four suggested ways to use AI. Have them explain which actions fit and disclose one use in a sentence.

Example. When layout feedback is allowed, keep the interpretation yours and decline a rewritten interpretation.

Look for. They can stay within the boundary and describe the specific effect AI had on their work.

Say thisRead the assignment’s permission before you open the tool.

Verification

Vocabulary. Supported, contradicted, not established

Fact Detective · Framework example

On paper. Give pairs a fictional source note and four chatbot claims. Have them sort the claims and fix or withhold the weak ones.

Example. Compare every table row with the original notes, preserve missing values and calculate the subtotal yourself.

Look for. They can show their evidence and change or withhold work when it does not support the claim.

Say thisShow me the source or test, then tell me what it proves.
  1. 5 min

    Opening

    Teacher. Show a fictional task with a tempting shortcut. Ask what AI is allowed to contribute.

    Students. Write the goal and what must stay with you.

    Say thisWhich choice still belongs to the student?
  2. 7 min

    Teach

    Teacher. Model today’s competency with its vocabulary and example. Think aloud about what you would check or change.

    Students. Identify the point where the student makes a decision.

    Say thisShow me where the person takes responsibility for the next move.
  3. 20 min

    Practice

    Teacher. Assign the matching station or its paper exercise. Ask students to explain the reason for one choice.

    Students. Work the scenario, revise your response if needed and compare it with a partner’s.

    Say thisWhat would you do first, and why?
  4. 8 min

    Check

    Teacher. On days 2–4, collect one scenario response. On day 5, give the suggested quiz (15 minutes, with practice shortened as above).

    Students. Give your response independently. Point to a detail from the task or source.

    Say thisName the competency and why your action fits the situation.
  5. 5 min

    Exit

    Teacher. Have students revisit one response through another competency. On day 5, close with a Crossroads example or its paper equivalent.

    Students. Write a next action that uses two competencies in the same situation.

    Say thisWhen you use AI, keep all four competencies in view.

Check for understanding

Suggested quiz · About 15 minutes. Use the results to plan reteaching. Use the formal foundation check for a Permit decision.

Printable student copyTeacher answer key
Preview the quiz questions
  1. Objective 1 · Prompting

    You want help rehearsing for a debate. The tool writes your opening statement. Write the next instruction you would give it.

  2. Objective 1 · Prompting

    You asked ‘Help with my graph’ and got a chart about a different subject. What three details would make the next prompt useful?

  3. Objective 2 · Data & Privacy

    A spreadsheet has classmates’ birthdays. Your task only needs counts by month. What would you give an approved tool?

  4. Objective 2 · Data & Privacy

    A photo has no names, but a timetable and a locker number are visible. You are unsure whether you may upload it. What do you do?

  5. Objective 3 · Ethics

    The assignment allows AI to test you on key terms. It offers to write the conclusion. What do you do?

  6. Objective 3 · Ethics

    You got permission to have AI suggest an outline. You used one of its sections. What would you tell your teacher?

  7. Objective 4 · Verification

    The class source says 42 students completed the survey. AI says 48 completed it. Is the AI claim supported, contradicted, or not established? What do you use?

  8. Objective 4 · Verification

    A handout lists the library’s hours. AI adds that the building was first opened in 1972. The handout says nothing about the date. What status fits the new claim?

  9. Objective 4 · Verification

    The tool reorganizes your prices into a table. All the rows look tidy. What check would catch a number it silently changed?

  10. Objective 2 · Data & Privacy

    Your group wrote a fictional interview but left in a real phone number from a reference sheet. You spot it before you upload. What is the next move?

  11. Objective 1 + 4 · Prompting and Verification

    You asked for an explanation of a map using your class legend. The response invents a symbol. Name two competencies and one action for each.

  12. Objective 2 + 3 · Data & Privacy and Ethics

    AI was approved for practice questions. Your partner suggests uploading a named draft so the tool can grade it. Name two competencies and one action for each.

Formal foundation check

When students are ready, use the classroom kit’s Independent foundation check (Form A or B) and fresh transfer card. Record Not yet, With guidance or Independent for each competency. Record the privacy and assignment-boundary gates as Pass, Not yet or Not observed. Use independent evidence and both gates when deciding readiness for supervised live practice. Keep those forms, keys and cases in the teacher kit.

Misconceptions and reteach

A name removed from a document means it is ready to share

Circle the details that could still identify someone. Start a new attempt with a fictional or minimal approved example.

Checking means asking the chatbot again

Give pairs one source and a contrary AI claim. Have them classify the claim, then rewrite or withhold it using the actual evidence.
