Lesson plan · Module 2 · Days 3–6

The four AI Core Competencies

Four 45-minute lessons, plus the suggested quiz. A suggested pace. Stretch or combine days to fit your class.

Goal Students can apply all four AI Core Competencies together, on paper, before they open a live tool.

Learning objectives

  1. Students will be able to use Prompting to fit the help to the goal, context and boundary, and redirect it when needed.
  2. Students will be able to apply Data & Privacy by choosing minimal approved input and stopping when approval is uncertain.
  3. Students will be able to apply Ethics by following the assignment’s permission and describing AI’s help honestly.
  4. Students will be able to use Verification to sort important claims as supported, contradicted or not established.

Key vocabulary

AI Core Competencies · Prompting · Data & Privacy · Ethics · Verification · Assignment boundary

Day 3 · Prompting

TimeStepTeacherStudentsSay this
5 minOpening
Name today’s Core Competency: Prompting		Say it plainly first: “Your words matter.” Then put one short assignment and its permission on the board. Teach today’s words: goal (what you need), context (what helps AI respond), boundary (what the tool may do).Underline the task’s learning target and name one decision you would make before using AI.“What are you learning here? What would you have to decide for yourself?”
7 minTeach
Think aloud through one example		Model this decision: ask for questions about an unclear pronoun when the reasoning must stay yours. Point to the line or source that supports it.Explain to a partner why the decision fits the task.“Here’s the information I used to make that choice. Which detail mattered?”
20 minPractice
Prompt Ladder		Open Prompt Ladder on student devices or the projector. Ask for reasons while students practice. Leave a little time for a second attempt.Rank opening prompts and explain which one gives fitting help. Compare your reason with a partner and revise one decision if needed.“Give AI its job and its limits. What part will you do yourself?”
8 minCheck
Compare evidence		Revisit two decisions from practice. Look for this: they give a goal and a boundary, explain why the help fits, and redirect when it drifts.Defend one choice using a direction or source and explain what you would change in a different situation.“Point to the part of the task or source that supports your choice.”
5 minExit
Exit with a decision and reason		Collect a response to a fresh short example. Use it to open the next day.Write what you would do and one piece of evidence for that decision.“I’m looking for your choice and the reason you can defend.”

Check for understanding Suggested quiz after Day 6: 12 scenario items, about 12–15 minutes. The formal check is the kit’s Independent foundation check.

Misconceptions and reteach

  • “I gave it a clear prompt. I’m done.” Say: “Look at the response. Does it still fit our goal and boundary?” Redo: Have students repair a prompt after the tool offers more help than the task allows.
  • “I erased the name. The rest is fine.” Say: “Look at how the details work together. What is the smallest approved input that will do the job?” Redo: Re-sort an invented note and supply a minimal substitute.
  • “I have a License, so AI is allowed.” Say: “Read the directions for this activity first.” Redo: Give two different assignment directions and ask students to justify what help is allowed on each.
  • “The source doesn’t say it’s wrong, so it must be true.” Say: “A source can leave a claim unsettled. What evidence would we need?” Redo: Sort the claims again and explain which to use, fix or hold back.
