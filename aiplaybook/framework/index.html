Framework / public overview

The AI Production Framework

A shared system for intentional, human-directed AI work: understand the technology, choose its role, follow a visible process, and remain responsible for the result.

The core stance

AI can extend human work. It does not inherit human judgment, authorship, or responsibility.

01

The framework at a glance

The AI Production Framework is a system for creating intentional, human-directed work with AI support. It begins with AI Literacy and combines habits that stay active, named cognitive roles for AI, a repeatable production workflow, clear human responsibility, and evidence of the decisions that shaped the work.

Its purpose is not to make people use AI more often. Its purpose is to help people decide when AI fits, use it with direction, and remain able to explain and stand behind the result.

AI Literacy → Core Competencies × AI Modes × Production Spiral × human responsibility and evidence
02

AI Core Competencies

The four AI Core Competencies are always on whenever AI is used. They apply across every AI Mode and every stage of the Production Spiral.

  • Prompting: express the goal, relevant context, constraints, sources, and AI role clearly.
  • Data & Privacy: minimize inputs, protect personal or sensitive information, and understand the tool’s data practices.
  • Ethics: consider permission, authorship, fairness, access, bias, impact, and whether the use should happen at all.
  • Verification: check important facts, reasoning, assumptions, sources, and changes through reliable methods or human expertise.
03

AI Modes

AI Modes describe the cognitive role a person asks AI to play in a particular moment. They provide shared language for choosing the kind of support that fits the task; they are not permissions to bypass responsibility.

  • Idea Mode: capture messy thinking, brainstorm possibilities, surface questions, and identify gaps.
  • Learning Mode: build understanding, clarify concepts, question explanations, and fill knowledge gaps.
  • Feedback Mode: critique, compare, or review work a person has already begun.
  • Tool Mode: organize, transform, format, build, or produce under clear human direction.
  • Practice Mode: rehearse, simulate, stress-test, and challenge reasoning or performance.
People may use one Mode, sequence several Modes, or combine Modes when the choice is intentional and explainable.
04

Production Spiral

Pre-Production → Production → Post-Production → Publish → Reflect

The Spiral gives AI-supported work a visible backbone. Pre-Production defines the task, human goal, AI plan, inputs, constraints, and verification route. Production is where people prompt, think, draft, and revise. Post-Production verifies, corrects, and challenges the work. Publish pairs the final artifact with useful process evidence. Reflect feeds what was learned into the next Pre-Production cycle.

The stages anchor the workflow without pretending that real work is perfectly linear. A person may revisit earlier decisions as evidence and constraints change.
05

Shared decision sequence

This sequence separates permission from purpose. It begins with the task, establishes the human boundary, and only then selects the cognitive role and environment for AI.

  • Name the task and learning goal.
  • Check which AI use the task allows, including no AI.
  • State what must remain your work.
  • Choose the AI Mode or Modes.
  • Write the prompt and provide useful context.
  • Choose an approved tool or environment.
  • Use the Production Spiral to work, check, and revise.
  • Keep Process Evidence and reflect on your decisions.
06

Process evidence keeps thinking visible

Process Evidence shows how you used AI: what AI contributed, how you directed it, what you checked, revised, or rejected, and which final decisions remained yours. Keep the record that fits the task.

  • An Evidence Log records key AI decisions, Mode choices, changes, and verification or ethics notes.
  • A Curated Chatlog selects meaningful conversation turns instead of requiring every token.
  • Drafts, before-and-after artifacts, screenshots, source checks, or a short process note may provide additional evidence.
  • The amount of evidence should fit the task. The goal is visibility and reflection—not surveillance or paperwork for its own sake.
07

Two expressions of the same system

  • Teacher-directed use: educators bring domain expertise, verified sources, classroom context, privacy-safe evidence, and professional constraints. AI may help execute or organize the work, but the teacher owns the instructional decision.
  • Student AI production: students plan before prompting, choose and sequence AI Modes, apply all four Core Competencies, verify output, publish useful evidence, and reflect on what remained their work.
Teachers can responsibly offload tasks when they do not offload responsibility. Students can use powerful support when the process strengthens rather than replaces learning.
08

A short example

A student creating an evidence-based explainer begins in Pre-Production by defining the task, identifying what must remain human, choosing Idea Mode and Learning Mode, gathering course sources, and naming a verification plan. During Production, the student may use Feedback Mode on an original draft and Tool Mode for a clearly directed transformation. In Post-Production, the student checks claims and uses Practice Mode to defend the reasoning. The final artifact is published with selected process evidence and a reflection.

That sequence is an example, not a required script. The appropriate AI Modes and evidence depend on the task, learner readiness, and local expectations.

09

What this public page includes

This page provides the shared architecture and language needed to understand the Playbook. It is not the complete implementation system.

Detailed rubrics, scoring mechanics, Evidence Log specifications, credential requirements, facilitator guidance, templates, and turnkey activities belong in the deeper learning and implementation materials.
