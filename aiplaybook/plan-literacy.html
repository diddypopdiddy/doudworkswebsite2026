AI Production Teacher Playbook

AI Literacy

Printable lesson plan

Goal Establish a shared vocabulary and a shared understanding, so the class can talk about AI without the myths.

Learning objectives

  1. Students will be able to define the key vocabulary in their own words.
  2. Students will be able to explain that a model learns patterns from examples and then makes predictions.
  3. Students will be able to place today’s chat tools in a short history of rules, machine learning, neural networks and generative AI.
  4. Students will be able to name one limit of AI output and explain why it needs checking.

Key vocabulary

AI · Predict · Classify · Recommend · Generate · Machine learning · Training and training data · Neural network · Generative AI · Language model · Prompt · Token

Materials

AI Literacy slide deck · Workbook Chapter 1 · The Quiz Board · Foundations Student Workbook

Day 1 · Teach it

TimeStepTeacherStudentsSay this
5 minOpening
Where did you meet AI before first period?		Collect examples such as recommendations, spam filters, maps, photo search and autocomplete. Ask what goes in and what comes out.Give one example and sort it as predict, classify, recommend or generate. Explain the choice to a partner.“A chatbot is one kind of AI. What did this system do with its input?”
12 minTeach
Train and test a classifier in Teachable Machine		Project the two-class demo with objects only: no student faces, no voices, no sign-in. Introduce each vocabulary word when students see its job.Predict the label before each test. Explain what the training examples helped the model do.“We gave it examples and a set of labels. What can it do when none of those labels fit?”
10 minTeach
From a classifier to a language model		Project the picture below. Then write two sentence stems, such as “Snow usually feels ___” and “The coolest weekend plan is ___”, and let the class be the model.Offer likely next pieces of text and compare them. Identify the prompt and a possible token. Say which stem had one likely answer and which had many.“That model predicts a label. A chat tool predicts the next piece of text. Same idea, much bigger.”
10 minPractice
Four steps of history		Put the four steps along the board. Hand out or read aloud everyday examples, such as a calculator, a spam filter, photo search and a chat tool.Place each example on the timeline and defend the choice. Explain how learning from examples differs from writing fixed rules.“Here’s the short version. These ideas overlap, and tools still use more than one approach.”
4 minCheck
Three quick questions		Cold-call, no notes: What is training data? What does classify mean? What does a language model predict?Answer in your own words and point to the moment in class that showed it.“Use your own words. Where did we see that happen today?”
4 minExit
Exit ticket		Collect one line from everyone. Use it to choose which words to revisit at the start of Day 2.Write one thing that surprised you and what showed it to you.“What surprised you today? Point to the part of the lesson that helped you see it.”

Day 2 · Practice it and check it

TimeStepTeacherStudentsSay this
5 minOpening
Say it without your notes		Put four of yesterday’s words on the board. Choose the ones the exit tickets showed were shaky.Define two words to a partner, then give an example of each from the demo.“No notes. Tell your partner what it means and where we saw it.”
8 minTeach
Myth or fact?		Read the six myths one at a time and ask for a reason with each vote. Land on the reality every time.Vote, then point to something from the demo or the picture that supports your call.“What did we see yesterday that helps you make that call?”
15 minPractice
Play the Quiz Board		Open Workbook Chapter 1 on the projector and run it in 2–4 teams. This is practice, so talk through every miss.Answer as a team and explain one answer in your own words before the next clue.“Before I reveal it, tell us why your team picked that.”
12 minCheck
Give the suggested quiz		Hand out the student copy. Allow about 10 minutes and collect it before any discussion.Complete the ten items on your own.“Use a concrete example when you explain why you would check an AI response.”
5 minExit
Go over what was missed		Use the answer key to review the two items most students missed. Each item is tagged to an objective, so a miss tells you what to reteach.Correct one answer and write why the right one fits.“Which word would have helped you answer that one?”

Check for understanding Suggested quiz: 10 items, about 10 minutes, on Day 2. Student copy and teacher answer key are separate printables.

Misconceptions and reteach

  • “I corrected it, so now the model knows.” Say: “It can use that correction while answering in this chat. Training changes the model itself.” Redo: Have students explain which part of the classifier activity was training and which part was testing.
  • “It sounded sure, so it must be right.” Say: “Even a confident label can be wrong. What evidence could we compare it with?” Redo: Revisit the unfamiliar object. Have students name what they observed and how they checked the label.
  • “Rules and machine learning are the same.” Say: “A rule tells the system what to do. Training examples help a model learn a pattern.” Redo: Have one pair sort with a written rule and another sort from examples, then compare.
