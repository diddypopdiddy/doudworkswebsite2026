Your subject

English language arts.

Reading and writing with AI in the room, without giving up student voice.

One idea per ModeLicense examples

Ground rules

Before you plan anything

Students still

Students interpret the text, choose the evidence, make the argument and write the final language in their own voice.

Road closed when

The goal is first-draft voice, independent reading, original interpretation or live discussion.

Check it by

Go back to the text. Check every quotation. Have the student explain their writing choices out loud.

Have students keep

The full conversation, plus notes, annotations, original drafts and the revisions that mattered.

Watch for: AI can flatten a student's voice, invent quotations or make a weak reading sound polished.

Watch for: No identifiable students or unpublished personal writing in a tool that isn't approved.

Guided practice · Module 3

One idea for each Mode

Each one is a single guided-practice cycle. Adapt the task to your unit.

Idea ModeStudent useNew

Find the gaps in a plan

Ask for questions, missing viewpoints or places that need evidence. Not a thesis, not a finished response.

BringThe student's own notes, first position or annotations.

Learning ModeStudent useNew

Unpack a hard word or passage

Ask for a simpler explanation or a comparison, say it back in your own words, then return to the assigned text.

BringThe assigned text, a first attempt at reading it and class materials to check against.

Feedback ModeStudent useNew

Get feedback on a draft

Ask about one part of the rubric. Accept, reject or revise each suggestion in your own words.

BringA complete student-written first draft and the rubric.

Practice ModeStudent useNew

Rehearse the discussion

Have AI take a reading of the text that disagrees with yours and press you one question at a time. Answer with evidence from the text before it responds.

BringYour claim, the text and two pieces of evidence you chose yourself.

Tool ModeStudent useNew

Organize your own notes

Turn your annotations into an outline or evidence chart using only what you wrote. Compare it line by line with your notes. Nothing gets added.

BringYour own annotations and the format you want.

Suggestions to adapt for your unit. Give students a fresh assessment task after any guided rehearsal.

Suggested worked brief

The two-minute book pitch

Task
Write and deliver a two-minute pitch persuading next year's class to read, or skip, a text you studied this term.
Only source
Use this fictional class excerpt and your own annotations. ‘On the last day the library stayed open, Mara left the brass key on the returns cart. She had carried it for six years. At the bus stop she reached into the pocket where it used to be, then walked back to hold the door for the next person.’
What AI is permitted to do
Use the approved tool and source. Use and document all five Modes in the order that fits your task. The options below show where help could fit. Choose your own prompts.
  • Idea ModeCompare a few angles for the pitch before choosing one.
  • Learning ModeClear up a passage or term, then go back to the text.
  • Feedback ModeCritique the draft against the rubric. No rewriting.
  • Practice ModeRehearse answering a skeptical listener.
  • Tool ModeTurn the finished script into cue cards, nothing added.
What stays yours
The claim, the evidence chosen from the text and every sentence of the pitch.
Subject learning assessed separately
Clarity of the recommendation, connection between two quoted details and the interpretation, and delivery for next year’s class.
What to hand in
The finished work, the full, dedicated task conversation, and a short explanation of what each Mode contributed, what you accepted or rejected, and what you verified.
Privacy
Use the excerpt and your own class annotations only. Keep names, reader records and classmates’ drafts out of the conversation.
Fresh transfer task
A new short passage and a one-paragraph response brief.

Another starting point

Editorial for a real reader

Write a 400-word editorial on a school or community question for the school paper, using two class-provided articles as your only sources.

Source
Two articles you choose and hand out.
What stays with the student
The position, the reasoning and every sentence.
