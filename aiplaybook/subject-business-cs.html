Ground rules

Before you plan anything

Students still

Students define the requirements, make the tradeoffs, choose the implementation and defend the final decision.

Road closed when

Students are being assessed on independent coding, system design or a high-stakes decision.

Check it by

Run the code. Test the edge cases. Check the data, sources and licenses.

Have students keep

The full conversation, plus decision notes, original and revised code, test results and source checks.

Watch for: AI can invent evidence, miss security problems or produce code that only looks correct.

Watch for: No confidential code, customer information, credentials or protected data in a tool that isn't approved.

Guided practice · Module 3

One idea for each Mode

Each one is a single guided-practice cycle. Adapt the task to your unit.

Idea ModeStudent useNew

Map the options before you build

Ask for three genuinely different approaches to the problem, with one tradeoff each. Pick or combine, and say why.

BringThe problem statement, the user and your own first idea.

Learning ModeStudent useNew

Understand an error or a concept

Name the error or concept, say what you think it means and ask for a hint, not a fix. Explain it back and apply it to a fresh example.

BringYour own attempt and the exact point of confusion.

Feedback ModeStudent useNew

Pressure-check a pitch or plan

Share your draft pitch, plan or pseudocode with the rubric. Ask for two gaps and the effect of each. No rewrite. You decide what changes.

BringA finished first draft and the criteria.

Practice ModeStudent useNew

Test a prototype

Ask AI for failure cases, unusual users or changing conditions. Predict what will happen, then test the strongest ones.

BringA clear user, a few requirements and a working idea or prototype.

Tool ModeStudent useNew

Clean up code or data

Reformat student-written code, data or project material without handing over the main design decision. Then test the result.

BringStudent-created material, the format you want and a way to test it.

License examples

Suggestions to adapt for your unit. Give students a fresh assessment task after any guided rehearsal.

Suggested worked brief

Pitch a fix

Task
A fictional small business has a customer problem. Propose one solution in a one-page pitch with a simple budget.
Only source
Use this fictional case card. A bakery gets 30 pickup orders on Saturdays. Six customers waited more than eight minutes last week. There is one register and a budget of $80. An extra sign costs $18, a numbered shelf costs $45 and printed tickets cost $12. Staff can spend ten minutes setting up.
What AI is permitted to do
Use the approved tool and source. Use and document all five Modes in the order that fits your task. The options below show where help could fit. Choose your own prompts.
  • Idea ModeCompare solution options and their tradeoffs.
  • Learning ModeNail a concept the case needs, such as break-even.
  • Feedback ModeCheck the pitch against the rubric.
  • Practice ModeRehearse investor questions.
  • Tool ModeFormat the budget table. Totals are checked by hand.
What stays yours
The recommendation, the tradeoffs and the numbers.
Subject learning assessed separately
A solution addresses the customer problem, fits the budget and staff constraint, and states how the bakery could check if waits improve.
What to hand in
The finished work, the full, dedicated task conversation, and a short explanation of what each Mode contributed, what you accepted or rejected, and what you verified.
Privacy
Use the fictional case and costs. Keep real sales, customer records and account data out of the tool.
Fresh transfer task
The same case with one constraint changed.

Another starting point

Build it and document it

Write a small program or spreadsheet model that meets a short spec, with a test log and a README explaining your design choices.

Source
The spec and the course reference.
What stays with the student
The design, logic they can explain line by line and the tests.
