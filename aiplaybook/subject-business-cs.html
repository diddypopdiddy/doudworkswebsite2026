Business and computer science.

Testing code, decisions and prototypes with AI, without hiding the reasoning.

Ground rules

Before you plan anything

Students still

Students define the requirements, make the tradeoffs, choose the implementation and defend the final decision.

Road closed when

Students are being assessed on independent coding, system design or a high-stakes decision.

Check it by

Run the code. Test the edge cases. Check the data, sources and licenses.

Have students keep

The full conversation, plus decision notes, original and revised code, test results and source checks.

Watch for: AI can invent evidence, miss security problems or produce code that only looks correct.

Watch for: No confidential code, customer information, credentials or protected data in a tool that isn't approved.

Suggestion · Worked brief

Pitch a fix

Task
A fictional small business has a customer problem. Propose one solution in a one-page pitch with a simple budget.
Your only source
Use this fictional shop case. Six of the last 20 pickup orders were delayed because staff could not find a packed order. The shop can spend up to $120 on a one-week pilot. A rack costs $65, reusable numbered tags cost $15, and a tablet stand costs $40. The shop already has a tablet and an approved spreadsheet. Staff have 30 minutes to learn the new process.
AI is permitted for
  • IdeaCompare solution options and their tradeoffs.
  • LearningNail a concept the case needs, such as break-even.
  • FeedbackCheck the pitch against the rubric.
  • PracticeRehearse investor questions.
  • ToolFormat the budget table. Totals are checked by hand.

Use each AI Mode purposefully. Choose your own prompts and order.

Yours and assessed
Make the recommendation, map how an order moves from packing to pickup, calculate the budget and define what you would measure in the pilot. Check every price and constraint against the case.
Assessed separately
Business and computer science: fit to the problem, feasible workflow, correct budget and a measurable way to judge the pilot. Review responsible AI use separately.
Hand in
Your one-page pitch, process diagram and itemized budget, the full, dedicated task conversation, and an explanation of what you accepted or rejected, what you checked and how you developed the work.
Privacy
Use approved task material and your own work. Keep names, identifiable images and personal information out of the tool. Submit through your private class route.
