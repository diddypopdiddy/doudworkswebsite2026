Science.

AI in science, without replacing observation, data or experiments.

One idea per ModeLicense examples

Ground rules

Before you plan anything

Students still

Students observe, measure, handle materials, choose the method, interpret the data and build the scientific argument.

Road closed when

Students are observing, collecting data, running an experiment or making their first interpretation of results.

Check it by

Inspect the method, raw data, calculations and assumptions. Compare the claim with trusted sources and real observations.

Have students keep

The full conversation, plus raw observations, data, methods, predictions, calculations and revised claims.

Watch for: AI can invent data, hide uncertainty and make weak causal claims sound certain.

Watch for: A simulation is not an experiment.

Guided practice · Module 3

One idea for each Mode

Each one is a single guided-practice cycle. Adapt the task to your unit.

Idea ModeStudent useNew

Turn an observation into questions

Turn something students actually observed into possible questions, variables or next investigations. Then choose one worth testing.

BringA real observation, a notebook entry and the practical limits of the investigation.

Learning ModeStudent useNew

Compare explanations

Ask for an analogy or another explanation, say it back, then compare it with trusted scientific sources.

BringA first attempt at explaining it and the course text or lab evidence.

Feedback ModeStudent useNew

Check a lab conclusion

Share your claim, evidence and reasoning draft. Ask where the reasoning doesn't follow from the data. Revise it yourself. AI never supplies data.

BringYour draft conclusion, your real data and the rubric.

Practice ModeStudent useNew

Try a labeled simulation

Change one condition, predict what may happen and name what a real experiment would still need to test.

BringThe concept, known conditions, a prediction and a clear line between model and observation.

Tool ModeStudent useNew

Format your data table

Organize raw measurements into a clean table with units. Missing values stay missing. Work out the averages yourself.

BringYour raw data, exactly as recorded.

Suggested worked brief

Explain our lab to next year's class

Task
Write a one-page lab summary with a diagram that explains what these trials showed and how sure you can be.
Only source
Use these fictional trials and your class definition of a fair test. A ramp at 10 cm height produced travel distances of 43, 45 and 42 cm. At 20 cm the distances were 71, 75 and 72 cm. The same ball, ramp and surface were used. The investigator released the ball by hand.
What AI is permitted to do
Use the approved tool and source. Use and document all five Modes in the order that fits your task. The options below show where help could fit. Choose your own prompts.
  • Idea ModeCompare ways to present the finding.
  • Learning ModeFirm up the concept behind the result.
  • Feedback ModeTest the reasoning against the data.
  • Practice ModeField questions from a skeptical reader.
  • Tool ModeBuild a clean data table with units. Averages are worked by hand.
What stays yours
The claim, the reading of the data and the stated limits.
Subject learning assessed separately
The claim matches the measurements, the labeled diagram helps explain the procedure, and the student identifies a limit relevant to the strength of the claim.
What to hand in
The finished work, the full, dedicated task conversation, and a short explanation of what each Mode contributed, what you accepted or rejected, and what you verified.
Privacy
Use the supplied measurements or an approved, de-identified notebook excerpt. Keep student names, photos and raw class records in school systems.
Fresh transfer task
A small new data set and a claim to evaluate.
