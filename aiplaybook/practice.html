Guided practice in each AI Mode (Permit)

A suggested pace. Stretch or combine days to fit your class.

Total time

Live student tool

Guided practice in the Framework

Goal

Students use each AI Mode on a real task while you supervise.

  • Idea Mode
  • Learning Mode
  • Feedback Mode
  • Practice Mode
  • Tool Mode

Each AI Mode has its own short page and a printable lesson plan. Conversation hand-in guide

Learning objectives

  1. Students will be able to use each of the five AI Modes to support a real class task.
  2. Students will be able to apply all four AI Core Competencies while directing and checking the help.
  3. Students will be able to complete the human finish and submit the full, dedicated task conversation.
  4. Students will be able to explain one decision from the conversation and act on feedback.

Key vocabulary

Permit
The stage for eligibility, foundations and supervised practice across the AI Modes.
Guided practice
Design, Model, Supply, Engage, Share and Review, with the teacher supervising.
The human finish
The decision, explanation, check or approval you do yourself before using the result.
The full, dedicated task conversation
A complete record of the exchange for one task. A portfolio across tasks is optional.

Lesson flow

  1. TeacherDesignPick the AI Mode and the task.
  2. TeacherModelShow how you direct the help.
  3. TeacherSupplyHand out a starter prompt.
  4. StudentsEngageDirect the conversation and do the work.
  5. StudentsShareHand in the full, dedicated task conversation.
  6. TeacherReviewRead, discuss and record the mark.

Opening

  1. Before class · 5 min in class

    Design

    Teacher
    Choose one AI Mode and a task from your subject. Name what AI may do and the thinking you will assess. Confirm tool eligibility and the private hand-in route.
    Students
    Identify what your AI Mode may do and what you will do yourself.
    Say thisToday this AI Mode has one job. What part stays with you?

Teach

  1. 5 min

    Model

    Teacher
    Think aloud using a conversation you saved in Module 0. Show one redirect and the human finish.
    Students
    Identify where the teacher made a choice or checked a result.
    Say thisWatch the response. Where do I need to steer it? What do I keep for myself?
  2. 2 min

    Supply

    Teacher
    Give the starter on the relevant AI Mode page and the small set of approved sources.
    Students
    Replace the brackets with details your task actually needs.
    Say thisUse only the source and context needed for this job.

Practice

  1. 20 min

    Engage

    Teacher
    Circulate and ask for reasons. Note the exact wording of any cue that helped the learner make a decision.
    Students
    Direct one dedicated conversation, check important results and complete the work.
    Say thisWhat will you use, change or hold back, and why?

Check

  1. 3 min

    Share

    Teacher
    Show the class its approved private hand-in route.
    Students
    Submit the full, dedicated task conversation and your task work.
    Say thisCheck that your hand-in starts with your first prompt and includes every turn.
  2. 5 min · finish reviewing after class

    Review

    Teacher
    Use the AI Mode rubric. Look at the opening request, a decision or redirect and the human finish, then read more wherever evidence is unclear.
    Students
    Point to the turn that shows an important decision or check.
    Say thisWhich part of the conversation shows the choice you made?

Exit

  1. 5 min

    Plan the next attempt

    Teacher
    Give one specific piece of feedback. Use a volunteered or made-up excerpt to talk through a choice with the class.
    Students
    Name one thing you will carry into the next AI Mode or try again.
    Say thisName one action you’ll take next time and what you’ll check.

Hand in the conversation

Use the route your school approved. Hand in the full, dedicated task conversation, from the first prompt through the human finish. A screenshot of one part leaves out evidence. Never post a link that anyone on the internet can open.

These steps are written to students and describe the web apps. Each tool has a student handout to print or project. School settings determine which options students see. Check the recipient and the sharing audience before submitting.

Gemini

School-managed Google Workspace
  1. In Gemini on the web, check that you’re in your school account, then open the finished task conversation.
  2. Below a response, select Share and then Share conversation. Use the Drive sharing dialog. If you only see a public-link option, close it and use the PDF hand-in.
  3. In the Drive sharing dialog, keep General access set to Restricted. Add your teacher’s school email and choose Viewer if there’s a role choice.
  4. Select Send or Share to grant access. Submit the restricted link in your class system when directed. It’s a snapshot, so share after your last turn.

Who can open it Only signed-in accounts you granted access to under your school’s Drive settings. If your teacher can’t open it, check their address or hand in a PDF.

Personal Google account
  1. Open the finished task conversation. Use your teacher’s approved PDF hand-in. Use the PDF steps in Any tool.
  2. Hand in the file through your class system. Gemini’s personal Share conversation option creates a public link. Leave it unused for classwork.

Who can open it The people your class system gives access to the submission.

ChatGPT

School-managed ChatGPT workspace
  1. Open the finished task conversation in your school’s ChatGPT Edu, Enterprise or Business workspace. Check the selected workspace and confirm your teacher is in it.
  2. Select Share from the chat header. Review the preview for the full task, from your first prompt to the final turn.
  3. If workspace sharing is approved for your class, select Create link or Copy link, as shown. Paste it into the private assignment submission.
  4. If sharing is off, your teacher is outside the workspace, or the class requires individual access, hand in a PDF. Use the PDF steps in Any tool.

Who can open it Eligible members of the same workspace who get the link. Access can’t be limited to one recipient. Workspace links can include later messages, so keep this conversation dedicated to the task.

Personal ChatGPT account
  1. Open the complete task conversation. Use your teacher’s approved PDF hand-in. Use the PDF steps in Any tool.
  2. Upload the file in your class system. A ChatGPT shared link from a personal account is open to anyone who has it.

Who can open it The people your class system gives access to the submission.

Student handout for ChatGPT

Last checked 21 November 2026. OpenAI’s conversation sharing guide

Claude

School-managed Claude Team or Enterprise
  1. Open the complete chat in your school organization at claude.ai. Select Share at the top right.
  2. Under People with access, add only your teacher’s school email. Keep general access limited to invited people. Select Send.
  3. Follow your class hand-in instructions. The teacher opens the invitation using the same email. If you add turns, use Update shared chat before handing it in.
  4. If your school disables sharing or the teacher’s account can’t be invited, use the approved PDF hand-in. Use the PDF steps in Any tool.

Who can open it The people you invite, using the invited address. School settings control whether invitations can go outside the organization.

Personal Claude account
  1. If the school approves this account and the teacher can open a Claude invitation, open the full chat and select Share at the top right.
  2. Add the teacher’s email under People with access, leave general access restricted and select Send. Keep public sharing off.
  3. Follow the classroom hand-in instructions. If the invitation route is unavailable or unapproved, hand in a PDF. Use the PDF steps in Any tool.

Who can open it An invited person signed in with the matching email can view the snapshot. They may need a Claude account. A public link would let anyone with the link view it.

Student handout for Claude

Any tool

School-managed or personal account
  1. Open the full, dedicated task conversation. Load earlier turns, expand long replies and check that the first prompt and final turn are visible.
  2. On a computer, choose File → Print (Ctrl+P or Command+P). Choose Save as PDF, or PDF → Save as PDF on a Mac. Keep all pages.
  3. Open the PDF and check that every turn is legible and in order. If the preview misses turns, copy the full text into an approved document, label each speaker and save that as a PDF.
  4. Upload the PDF to the assignment in your school’s class system. If you can’t create a PDF on your device, ask your teacher for an accessible way to submit the full transcript.

Who can open it The people your school allows to open your assignment submission. Follow the same private route whether the tool was school-managed or personal.

Student handout for Any tool

Check for understanding

Review the conversation

Use the rubric for the AI Mode you chose. Look for Purposeful AI Mode use and evidence of Prompting, Data & Privacy, Ethics and Verification. Record Not yet · With guidance · Independent and the exact cue, if you supplied one. Use the rubric for one student, or the class marking sheet to mark everyone on one page.

Once you review a finished cycle, add its AI Mode mark to the Permit and record the rubric level beside it. The mark is yours to give. Repeat the cycle for each AI Mode.

Misconceptions and reteach

“I kept only the useful response.”
Say thisThe record shows your setup, decisions and finish. Save all the turns for that task.

Redo Walk through the approved hand-in steps once with a fictional conversation.

“My first answer looks good. I’m finished.”
Say thisShow me the part you checked and the decision you made.

Redo Use the AI Mode rubric to identify a missing action and retry it on a short task.
