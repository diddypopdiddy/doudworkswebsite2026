Your subject

Mathematics.

AI in math class, without letting it do the thinking.

Ground rules

Before you plan anything

Students still

Students choose the method, show the work, check the answer and explain why it makes sense.

Road closed when

You need to see what a student can solve independently.

Check it by

Recalculate. Try another method. Check the units. Have the student explain the reasoning without AI.

Have students keep

The full conversation, plus the original attempt, shown work, corrections and the final explanation.

Watch for: AI can produce confident-looking math that is completely wrong.

Watch for: A polished answer is not proof the student understands it.

Suggestion · Worked brief

Which plan is the better deal?

Task
Compare two made-up pricing plans and recommend one for a described customer, with the math shown and a one-page explanation.
Your only source
Use this fictional recreation-center price sheet. Plan A costs $12 per month plus $2 per visit. Plan B costs $5 per month plus $3 per visit. No other fees apply. The customer expects eight visits next month, with a possible range of six to nine.
AI is permitted for
  • IdeaCompare ways to model it: table, graph or equation.
  • LearningGet a second explanation of a concept the task needs.
  • FeedbackFind the first step where the reasoning breaks.
  • PracticeHave AI challenge the recommendation with a different customer.
  • ToolFormat the finished table. Totals are worked by hand.

Use each AI Mode purposefully. Choose your own prompts and order.

Yours and assessed
Build a model for each plan, calculate costs and the break-even point yourself, and explain whether your recommendation changes over the customer’s possible range. Verify your numbers against the price sheet.
Assessed separately
Mathematics: accurate models and calculations, clear representations, and a recommendation that accounts for the range. Review responsible AI use separately.
Hand in
A one-page recommendation with your model, calculations and comparison, the full, dedicated task conversation, and an explanation of what you accepted or rejected, what you checked and how you developed the work.
Privacy
Use approved task material and your own work. Keep names, identifiable images and personal information out of the tool. Submit through your private class route.
