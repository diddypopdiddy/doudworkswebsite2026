Mathematics.

AI in math class, without letting it do the thinking.

Ground rules

Before you plan anything

Students still

Students choose the method, show the work, check the answer and explain why it makes sense.

Road closed when

You need to see what a student can solve independently.

Check it by

Recalculate. Try another method. Check the units. Have the student explain the reasoning without AI.

Have students keep

The full conversation, plus the original attempt, shown work, corrections and the final explanation.

Watch for: AI can produce confident-looking math that is completely wrong.

Watch for: A polished answer is not proof the student understands it.

Guided practice · Module 3

One idea for each Mode

Each one is a single guided-practice cycle. Adapt the task to your unit.

Idea ModeStudent useNew

Find more than one way in

Before solving, ask for three different strategies you could try, without carrying any of them out. Choose one and say why.

BringThe problem and your first thought about where to start.

Learning ModeStudent useNew

Get a second explanation

Ask for a visual, a simpler explanation or a similar example. Compare it with the class method.

BringA real first attempt and the exact point of confusion.

Feedback ModeStudent useNew

Find the step that went wrong

Share your full worked solution. Ask it to point to the first step where the reasoning breaks, without giving the answer. Fix it yourself.

BringComplete shown work. AI can be wrong about which step is wrong, so recalculate.

Practice ModeStudent useNew

Challenge the reasoning

Ask for a counterexample or a challenge question, then defend or revise your original method.

BringA method you chose, shown work and why you think it should work.

Tool ModeStudent useNew

Tidy your own data

Have AI lay out data you collected as a clean table. Compare every row with the original, keep missing values and work out the totals yourself.

BringYour own data and the layout you want.

License examples

Suggestions to adapt for your unit. Give students a fresh assessment task after any guided rehearsal.

Suggested worked brief

Which plan is the better deal?

Task
Compare two made-up pricing plans and recommend one for a described customer, with the math shown and a one-page explanation.
Only source
Use the teacher’s fictional plan sheet. Plan A costs $6 per month plus $3 per rental. Plan B costs $12 per month plus $1.50 per rental. The customer expects three rentals this month and could need six in a busy month. There are no other fees.
What AI is permitted to do
Use the approved tool and source. Use and document all five Modes in the order that fits your task. The options below show where help could fit. Choose your own prompts.
  • Idea ModeCompare ways to model it: table, graph or equation.
  • Learning ModeGet a second explanation of a concept the task needs.
  • Feedback ModeFind the first step where the reasoning breaks.
  • Practice ModeHave AI challenge the recommendation with a different customer.
  • Tool ModeFormat the finished table. Totals are worked by hand.
What stays yours
The model they choose, every calculation and the recommendation.
Subject learning assessed separately
Correct model and calculations, clear representations for both months and a reasoned recommendation that accounts for changes in the number of rentals.
What to hand in
The finished work, the full, dedicated task conversation, and a short explanation of what each Mode contributed, what you accepted or rejected, and what you verified.
Privacy
Use the fictional prices. Keep household spending, account details and payment information out of the tool.
Fresh transfer task
A new pair of plans with different numbers.
