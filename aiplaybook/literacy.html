Module 1 · Day 1

AI Literacy

A suggested pace. Stretch or combine days to fit your class.

MaterialsLesson flowCheck for understanding
At a glance
Time
55 minutes, or split across two classes
Live tool
Teacher demo only · paper alternative

Goal

Establish a shared vocabulary and a shared understanding, so the class can talk about AI without the myths.

Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Define the key vocabulary in your own words.
  2. Explain how a model learns patterns from examples and makes predictions.
  3. Place current chat tools in a short timeline of rules, machine learning, neural networks and generative AI.
  4. Name one limit of AI output and show how you would check it.

Key vocabulary

AI
Computer systems that perform tasks such as recognizing patterns or making predictions.
Predict
Estimate what is likely to come next or be true.
Classify
Assign an example to a category.
Recommend
Suggest an option based on patterns, preferences, or other inputs.
Generate
Produce something new, such as text, an image, or audio.
Machine learning
A family of methods in which a system learns patterns from examples.
Training and training data
Training is the process of learning patterns. Training data are the examples used.
Neural network
A kind of model that uses connected layers of calculations to learn patterns.
Generative AI
AI that learns patterns and uses them to produce new material.
Language model
A model that learns patterns in language and can predict pieces of text.
Prompt
The input or request you give a model.
Token
A piece of text the model works with, such as a word, part of a word, or punctuation.

Lesson flow

Teachable Machine demo

  1. Open Teachable Machine’s image project. On the teacher device, make two classes named Pen and Mug.
  2. Hold each object in front of the camera at several angles. Collect examples for one class at a time, then select Train Model.
  3. Use the preview to test another pen and mug. Ask what the model learned. Try a marker, a different background or an empty frame. Compare its label with what’s actually in view.

Use objects and a clear background. Work without signing in or saving to Drive. Google describes browser-based training. Check the current terms and your school’s approval before use.

Google’s Teachable Machine overview

On paper. Give groups labeled pictures of two categories and ask them to infer a rule. Show a new picture that breaks the rule. Add a missing kind of example and try again.

  1. 5 min

    Opening

    Teacher. Ask where students met AI before first period. Gather and sort examples by what the system did.

    Students. Offer an example and test whether predict, classify, recommend or generate describes it.

    Say thisWhat did the system actually do?
  2. 18 min

    Teach

    Teacher. Run the demonstration below in about ten minutes. Introduce training data when you collect examples and classification when the preview predicts a label. Then model next-token prediction with ‘We parked the car in the…’. Show the class timeline using the Framework screens.

    Students. Predict the model’s label before you test. Offer a likely next piece of text, change the context and predict again. Explain one difference between a classroom classifier and a chat tool.

    Say thisBoth use learned patterns. This model predicts a label. A language model can predict the next piece of text.
  3. 15 min

    Practice

    Teacher. Run a round of the Quiz Board and ask teams to defend an answer. Use paper prompts if the game is unavailable.

    Students. Give the term or action and say what example helped you decide.

    Say thisGive your answer, then tell us what the model was doing.
  4. 12 min

    Check

    Teacher. Start with one myth-or-fact discussion, then hand out the suggested quiz.

    Students. Complete the quiz independently.

    Say thisIf a response sounds sure, what can you do to see if it’s right?
  5. 5 min

    Exit

    Teacher. Read two exits and group responses by what needs reteaching.

    Students. Write one thing that surprised you and a check you could use this week.

    Say thisWhat will you look for the next time you see an AI answer?

  5. Objective 2 · Patterns and predictions

    A chatbot writes a confident answer. Its confidence alone can establish that the answer is correct. ________

  7. Objective 2 · Patterns and predictions

    A classifier that was only shown blue mugs might struggle to identify a red mug. ________

  8. Objective 4 · Limits and checking

    A model can generate a source title that sounds real even when the source cannot be found. ________

  9. Objective 3 · History

    Put these in the order from our class timeline. Generative AI, rules written by people, modern deep neural networks, early machine learning.

  10. Objective 4 · Limits and checking

    Give one reason to check an AI answer and name a source or action you could use to check it.

Misconceptions and reteach

The model is sure, so it must be right

Show a confident wrong label. Change the background or object and ask what additional training examples might help.

The model has one obvious next word

Try a second sentence frame. Give students two plausible tokens and have them explain how the context changes the prediction.
