Module 1 · Days 1–2

AI Literacy

A suggested pace. Stretch or combine days to fit your class.

MaterialsLesson flowCheck for understanding
At a glance
Time
Two 45-minute periods
Live tool
Teacher demo or paper activity

Goal

Explain how AI makes a prediction and use a class source to check an AI answer.

Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Explain how a model learns patterns from examples.
  2. Tell the difference between picking a label and predicting the next piece of text.
  3. Place today’s chat tools on a short AI timeline.
  4. Use a source to check a chatbot claim. Explain what you would keep or correct.

Key vocabulary

AI
Computer systems that perform tasks such as recognizing patterns or making predictions.
Predict
Estimate what is likely to come next or be true.
Classify
Assign an example to a category.
Recommend
Suggest an option based on patterns, preferences, or other inputs.
Generate
Produce something new, such as text, an image, or audio.
Machine learning
A family of methods in which a system learns patterns from examples.
Training and training data
Training is the process of learning patterns. Training data are the examples used.
Neural network
A kind of model that uses connected layers of calculations to learn patterns.
Generative AI
AI that learns patterns and uses them to produce new material.
Language model
A model that learns patterns in language and can predict pieces of text.
Prompt
The input or request you give a model.
Token
A piece of text the model works with, such as a word, part of a word, or punctuation.

Lesson flow

Teachable Machine demo

  1. On the teacher’s device, open Teachable Machine’s image project. Make two groups named Pen and Mug.
  2. Show a pen and a mug from several angles, one group at a time. Call these examples training data. Then select Train Model.
  3. Before each test, have students guess the label. Try a different pen or mug, then a spoon. Ask students to explain why a model that only knows the two labels still picks one.

Use objects only. Check school approval and the tool’s terms. Use the teacher device without signing in or saving the model. For a paper demo, give pairs labeled pen and mug pictures to study, then ask them to sort a new pen, mug, and spoon into the two groups.

Day 1 · From a prediction to a check

  1. 5 min

    Opening

    Teacher. Show slide 2. Ask students where they met AI today. Sort two examples by what goes in and what comes out.

    Students. Choose an example. Say whether it predicts, classifies, recommends or generates, and explain why.

    Say thisWhat did the system do with the information it got?
  2. 20 min

    Teach

    Teacher. Use slides 3–5. Run the pen, mug and spoon demo for about 10 minutes. Then ask for likely next pieces of text after “We parked the car in the…” Change the sentence to give a different clue.

    Students. Guess the label or next piece of text. Explain how both models use patterns and what each one predicts.

    Say thisThe first model picks a label. The chat model predicts the next piece of text.
  3. 10 min

    Practice

    Teacher. Use slide 7. Show a made-up AI answer that says a club meets Tuesday and a class notice that says Thursday. Model finding the exact part to check and using the notice to fix it.

    Students. Point to the words in the notice that tell you which day to use. Rewrite the answer.

    Say thisA confident answer still needs a check. What does the notice actually say?
  4. 7 min

    Check

    Teacher. Give each student the library example below. Collect their own response before discussing it.

    Students. Use the notice to check the chatbot answer. Write what you checked and what you would tell a classmate.

    Say thisShow the words you used to make your decision.
  5. 3 min

    Exit

    Teacher. Use the responses to choose a misconception for tomorrow. Look for the claim, the source detail, and a justified correction.

    Students. Tell a partner one thing a chatbot predicts and one thing you can do to check its answer.

    Say thisWhat can a person check that the chatbot’s confidence cannot tell you?

Check a chatbot answer

Class notice. The school library is open until 3:30 on normal school days. On Wednesdays it closes at 2:30.

Chatbot answer. “You can use the library until 3:30 on Wednesday.”

Write your answer. Which part of the chatbot’s answer would you check? Which words in the notice help you? Write the answer you would give a classmate.

Day 2 · Practice and check on your own

  1. 5 min

    Opening

    Teacher. Revisit the language from yesterday’s responses. Put training data, label, prompt, and token on the board.

    Students. Explain two words to a partner and point to an example from Day 1.

    Say thisWhere did we see that happen yesterday?
  2. 8 min

    Teach

    Teacher. Use slide 6 for a short timeline. Compare a fixed calculator rule, a spam filter, photo search, and a chat tool. Explain that the approaches overlap.

    Students. Place each example on the class timeline. Explain which uses fixed rules and which learns from examples.

    Say thisWhat did a person write as a rule, and what did the system learn from examples?
  3. 15 min

    Practice

    Teacher. Run a round of the Quiz Board or use its questions on paper. Have teams explain one answer. Revisit one misconception from Day 1.

    Students. Use a lesson example to explain your answer.

    Say thisWhich example helped you work that out?
  4. 12 min

    Check

    Teacher. Give the suggested quiz. The last item asks students to check a new chatbot answer with a source.

    Students. Answer on your own. Use the notice on the last question to show what you checked.

    Say thisPoint to the part of the source that changed or confirmed your answer.
  5. 5 min

    Exit

    Teacher. Use the answer key to discuss two common misses. Take the source-check responses into the Verification lesson in Module 2.

    Students. Fix one answer and write why you changed it.

    Say thisWhat will you look for the next time a chatbot gives you an answer?

Check for understanding

Look at the Day 1 library check and the last quiz question. Can each student name the claim, point to the source and correct the answer?

Preview the quiz questions
  1. Objective 1 · Vocabulary

    A music app suggests a playlist based on what you played last week. Which action is it taking? A. Classify   B. Recommend   C. Generate   D. Translate

  2. Objective 1 · Vocabulary

    What is a prompt? A. The input or request you give a model   B. The list of examples it learned from   C. A prediction score

  3. Objective 1 · Vocabulary

    What do we call the examples a model learns from? A. Prompts   B. Training data   C. Tokens

  4. Objective 1 · Vocabulary

    Which describes a token? A. A whole conversation   B. A source citation   C. A piece of text the model works with

  5. Objective 2 · Patterns and predictions

    A model trained on labeled photos puts a new picture in the ‘recycling bin’ category. Which action did it take? A. Train   B. Classify   C. Generate

  6. Objective 4 · Limits and checking

    A chatbot writes a confident answer. You can tell the answer is right because it sounds sure. ________

  7. Objective 2 · Patterns and predictions

    A classifier that was only shown blue mugs might struggle to identify a red mug. ________

  8. Objective 4 · Limits and checking

    A model can generate a source title that sounds real even when the source cannot be found. ________

  9. Objective 3 · History

    Put these in the order from our class timeline. Generative AI, rules written by people, modern deep neural networks, early machine learning.

  10. Objective 4 · Limits and checking

    Class notice: The chess club meets in Room 12 on Mondays and Room 18 on Tuesdays. A chatbot says, “Go to Room 12 on Tuesday.” What would you tell a friend? Show the words in the notice you used.

Misconceptions and reteach

The model sounds sure

Show the spoon result again. Compare it to what students see. Then give students a short answer and a notice to check.

The model always picks the same next word

Try a second sentence stem. Compare two likely next pieces of text and ask what clue changed the prediction.
