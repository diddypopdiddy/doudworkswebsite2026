Idea Mode in the Framework

Goal

Explore alternatives for a real task, then choose or adapt a direction and explain why it fits.

  1. Set upBring a starting idea
  2. Use AIExplore possibilities
  3. Think it throughCombine, redirect or select
  4. The human finishChoose and explain why

Learning objectives

  1. Students will be able to bring their own starting thought or question, audience and constraints.
  2. Students will be able to ask for distinct possibilities and tradeoffs within the task’s limits.
  3. Students will be able to question, refine, combine or reject alternatives.
  4. Students will be able to explain where their final idea came from and why it fits.

Key vocabulary

Idea Mode
Explore alternatives for a real task, then choose or adapt a direction and explain why it fits.
Alternative
A different way you could approach the task.
Tradeoff
Something you gain and something you give up with a choice.
The human finish
The decision, explanation, check or approval you do yourself before using the result.

Task ideas by subject
  • English language artsFind the gaps in a plan

    Ask for questions, missing viewpoints or places that need evidence. Not a thesis, not a finished response.

    Bring The student's own notes, first position or annotations.

  • HistoryAsk "what if?" questions

    Change one condition, predict possible effects and name what the scenario leaves out. Then choose the question worth investigating.

    Bring A verified account of what happened and a clear line between fact and speculation.

  • MathematicsFind more than one way in

    Before solving, ask for three different strategies you could try, without carrying any of them out. Choose one and say why.

    Bring The problem and your first thought about where to start.

  • ScienceTurn an observation into questions

    Turn something students actually observed into possible questions, variables or next investigations. Then choose one worth testing.

    Bring A real observation, a notebook entry and the practical limits of the investigation.

  • World languagesPlan what you want to say

    Ask for possible angles or questions around your topic. You choose, then write it in the target language yourself. Not a translated script.

    Bring Your topic, your level and the unit vocabulary.

  • Business and computer scienceMap the options before you build

    Ask for three genuinely different approaches to the problem, with one tradeoff each. Pick or combine, and say why.

    Bring The problem statement, the user and your own first idea.

  • Physical education and healthDesign a game variation

    Ask for three ways to change a game or warm-up for today's skill focus, with a tradeoff for each. Pick one, fit it to the real space and explain the choice.

    Bring The skill focus, group size, equipment and space.

  • Related artsFind a direction

    Surface possible themes, questions, audiences or limits. Then transform one through sketching, rehearsal or making.

    Bring The student's interests, references, first point of view or exploratory work.

Special education access notes and adult-use ideas

Lesson flow

The six steps of guided practice, and what Idea Mode changes in four of them.

  1. TeacherDesignPick the AI Mode and the task.
  2. TeacherModelShow how you direct the help.
  3. TeacherSupplyHand out a starter prompt.
  4. StudentsEngageDirect the conversation and do the work.
  5. StudentsShareHand in the full, dedicated task conversation.
  6. TeacherReviewRead, discuss and record the mark.

Design · students bring

A starting idea or question, the task goal, audience and constraints.

Model · show this

Compare two suggested directions with a constraint from your brief. Explain why you reject one, then show how you adapt the other.

Say thisThis one fits the time limit. What would I have to change to make it work for our audience?

Engage · students do this

Ask for choices and one tradeoff each. Compare them against a class constraint, redirect when needed, and explain your final choice in your own words.

Exit · ask this

AskWhich idea did you keep, change or reject? Name the constraint that guided you.

Supply, Share and Review work the same way in every AI Mode. See all six steps, or print the full plan for this session.

With guidance means a cue identifies the decision the student needs to make. Record the exact cue. Permitted AI help can still be part of independent work. The student must do the thinking being assessed.

Read the evidence in the conversation. Length, spelling, polish and AI praise do not show readiness. You give the mark and record the level.

Misconceptions and reteach
