Lesson plan · Module 0 · Day 0 · your prep

Use it yourself first

45-minute planning period. A suggested pace. Stretch or combine days to fit your class.

Goal Know how each AI Mode works for you, and where the tool falls short in your subject, before you teach it.

Learning objectives

  1. You will be able to use each of the five AI Modes once on your own planning.
  2. You will be able to name the human finish in each AI Mode.
  3. You will be able to find and document one error or assumption in your subject.
  4. You will be able to save conversations you can use to model the AI Modes.

Key vocabulary

AI Modes · The human finish · The full, dedicated task conversation

Materials

Choose work you already understand		Pick a lesson you will teach soon. Use the school-approved staff tool and material approved for that tool.Write down the course, learning goal, available time and material you can use safely.“I’m planning for this class. AI can support a task, and I’m responsible for the result.”
7 minTeach
Decide what you will hand off		Identify the small job the tool can do. Keep student decisions and records with you.Mark the material you can share and name the part you must do yourself.“If a detail is missing, flag it. I’ll supply it or leave it out.”
20 minPractice
Run one prompt in each of the five AI Modes		Fill in the brackets. Use a separate, dedicated conversation for each task. Redirect the help when it drifts.Save all five conversations and write a short note on the human finish in each.“Here’s the goal, what you can use and where your role ends. Wait for me to make the final decision.”
8 minCheck
Find where the tool falls short		Ask a question in your subject that you know cold. Compare the response to an approved source or a result you can reproduce. Mark where it is wrong, vague or overconfident.Save the exact claim, your check and the correction. If the first response holds up, test a common exception or missing detail. Use the example in Module 1.“Which part can I check myself? What should I hold back until I have evidence?”
5 minExit
Check it before students see it		Check facts, examples, calculations, accessibility, bias and tone against your curriculum and class. Revise or discard anything that misses the goal.Choose a conversation to model. Complete the self-check below.“I’ll show where I redirected the tool and what I did to finish the work.”

Check for understanding Teacher self-check: five yes-or-no statements.

Misconceptions and reteach

  • “I can use the plan as soon as it reads well.” Say: “Check what it asks students to learn and whether the examples hold up.” Redo: Compare one example line by line with your source and fix it yourself.
  • “I took the student’s name off. It’s ready to paste.” Say: “Class details can identify someone. Use a fictional example with only what the task needs.” Redo: Replace the record with a short, invented scenario.
